# Household Debt Pressure Index — Executive Brief
**Independent research | Mariia Nyaas | 1 November 2026**

### Executive question
Does improving aggregate leverage necessarily indicate improving household debt-service capacity?

### Evidence
SSB reports that aggregate debt relative to disposable income declined from 242.1% in H2 2021 to 221.9% at end-2024. Yet banking-sector interest expenses increased from 9.8% of disposable income in 2023 to 10.9% in 2024. September 2026 outstanding mortgage rates were 5.31%; in Q3 2026, 96.1% of mortgage balances were floating-rate.

### Assessment
These different-period aggregate measures suggest that a single leverage ratio is insufficient to characterise borrowing pressure. They do not establish which individual borrowers are distressed, nor do they support a causal estimate.

### Decision implications
1. Monitor multiple dimensions: outstanding leverage, actual debt-service cash flow, disposable-income buffer and interest-rate reset exposure.
2. Stress-test simultaneous income and interest-rate shocks; track response by relevant borrower segments.
3. Use verified loan-level payment behaviour and documented affordability policies before recommending underwriting changes.

### Evidence standard
Official SSB data supply macro reference values. The dashboard scenario is deterministic and hypothetical; it is not a validated early warning score. Consult the repository methodology and source links.
